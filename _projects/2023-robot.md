Our 12th grade robot is the one I'm most proud of. We didn’t make it to World’s like the year before, but I had almost complete control over it’s design and I’m proud of it’s macro-layout and it’s cleanness/finish. It had a passthrough intake-outtake path for the game pieces, angled vertical slides, and a belt fourbar linkage. If I could change this robot I would drive both slides with two pulleys that are geared together instead of driving it from one side to reduce binding.

The robot from 11th grade was great, especially during the early season, but we never ended up making any major changes after the first two competitions and we definitely left a lot on the table by not doing a major overhaul and instead adding small improvements. This seems to be a common way projects can slow down or even end, and it definitely also happened to Hatchet(the big drone).

For our 10th and 9th grade robots, I was not head builder during these seasons so I didn't contribute as much to them, but they were pretty cool and you can see how our team improved over the years and adopted more meta strategies. I did make the main gripper for the 9th grade robot (in Tinkercad lol) though, which was a cool first project.
